Engineering log · interactive disassembly · real CAD of the 2025 arm, version A

FeisarOne arm. Four joints. Five printed links.

Everything that moves on this arm came off a home 3D printer, including the planetary reducers that carry the torque. Scroll to take it apart link by link and reducer by reducer; drag to look around. Simulated geometry, not a manufacturing model.

01 · exploded

Five links, four reducers

The structure is printed ABS. Each joint drives through a planetary reducer printed with the link that holds it, so the motors stay small and the arm stays light enough to lift its own weight in open loop.

  • Motors3 × NEMA 17 · 1 × NEMA 23
  • Drivers · controllerTB6600 / DM556 · ESP32
  • Build5 weeks · July 2025

02 · link 0 · base

Base

Holds the J1 motor and the reducer that turns the whole arm. The 2025 base used a single 6816 bearing and had about 0.7° of backlash from a spreadsheet convention, roughly 8 mm at the end of the arm.

Inside, pulled apart along the axis: the NEMA 17, the sun, three planets in a carrier, the ring, the same again for the second stage, then the output bearing. Two stages of 1:7 make 1:49.

This is where the 2026 rebuild starts: the RPV1-B reducer, 1:41.33, generated tooth profiles, output on two 6010 bearings and an encoder reading the output directly. Nothing of it is printed yet.

  • Reducer, 2025RPV1 · 1:49
  • Reducer, 2026 designRPV1-B · 1:41.33

03 · link 1 · waist

Waist

Turns on J1 and carries the shoulder motor, the only NEMA 23 on the arm, 3 N·m through a second 1:49 RPV1. The orange parts are the RPV1 housings: the part of the project that has survived every rebuild since 2024.

Exploded inside: the NEMA 23 drives a second two-stage RPV1, sideways, straight into the upper arm.

  • AxisJ1 · ±170°
  • Shoulder driveNEMA 23 · 1:49

04 · link 2 · upper arm

Upper arm

Pitches on J2. In version A it includes the extension link that takes the reach to 681 mm; version B uses a short link and gives up 160 mm. Both were checked pose by pose against the table and against the other links.

Inside the arm, the roll drive: a NEMA 17 and a two-stage RPV2 planetary, smaller than the RPV1, with an 80 mm bearing at the output.

  • AxisJ2 · ±95°
  • ConfigurationsA extension · B short

05 · link 3 · elbow

Elbow

Rolls on J3, along the upper arm, through a two-stage 1:41.8 planetary on a NEMA 17. The elbow motor sits here too, which keeps the forearm light and the roll inertia low.

Exploded inside: the elbow's own two-stage RPV2, driven across the housing, with the forearm bolted to its output bearing.

  • AxisJ3 · ±180°
  • Roll driveNEMA 17 · 1:41.8

06 · link 4 · forearm

Forearm

Pitches on J4 through the largest ratio on the arm, a two-stage 1:80. It ends in the wrist flange: no wrist yet, no gripper yet. From the flange the arm reaches 681 mm out and 914 mm up.

  • AxisJ4 · ±137°
  • Elbow driveNEMA 17 · 1:80
  • Reach at the flange681 / 914 mm

07 · back together

Within the limits, nothing touches

The joint limits in the firmware come from this model: every combination of shoulder, elbow and roll on a 5° grid was tested for link-on-link contact and for the wrist dropping below the table. The motion here is the same pick-and-place used in the simulation videos.
